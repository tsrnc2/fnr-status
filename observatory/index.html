01 / In plain English

A student, a teacher, and a very strict rulebook.

Most AI systems can write an answer that sounds convincing. FNR explores something narrower and easier to test: can a model produce a correct chain of logical steps?

Think of the AI as a student solving a puzzle. It suggests one step at a time. A separate, rule-based checker plays the teacher: it verifies the proof instead of trusting the student's confidence. If the proof fails, it is not counted as correct.

The long-term hope is to develop AI tools that can reason more reliably and offer evidence for their answers. For now, this is a controlled research experiment using small, formal problems—not an AI that can solve any problem.

How a problem gets solved
01
Give the system a logic puzzle

A question with precise rules and an answer that can be checked.

02
Let the model suggest steps

It tries possible moves and builds a proof, much like showing work in math class.

03
Check every step independently

A separate checker accepts a valid proof or rejects it. Plausible wording is not enough.

02 / On the workbench

What the team is working on

Several pieces have to come together before a research prototype can become a reliable tool.

FOCUS 01

Teach one skill at a time

The model learns a specific logical move, faces new puzzles, then retakes old lessons to make sure it hasn't forgotten them.

FOCUS 02

Double-check the proofs

Researchers are testing independent proof-checking methods, including an experimental connection to Lean, a mathematical proof system.

FOCUS 03

Make training dependable

Training must survive memory problems, interruptions, restarts, and different computers without losing its verified progress.

04 / The road ahead

What still needs to be done

FNR has shown promising results in controlled exercises. These are the main steps between research progress and a trustworthy working system.

Complete the remaining logic topics and difficulty levels while proving that earlier skills are retained.

02

Have separate reviewers run the proof tests, check source code, challenge weak spots, and record their conclusions.

Finish external proof verification

05 / Common questions

A few things worth knowing.

No background in artificial intelligence or mathematics is needed to follow the project.

Is FNR another chatbot?

No. It's a focused experiment in teaching an AI model to find formal proofs. The goal isn't polished conversation; it's correct reasoning steps that independent software can check.

What does “passed a lesson” mean?

The model must solve several large batches of new, unseen test problems and still perform well on previously learned topics. Those are controlled lab results, not proof it can reason correctly about everything.

Why use a second checker?

AI can produce a confident but wrong answer. A separate proof checker enforces precise rules, so the model cannot earn credit just by sounding right. That checker also needs its own testing and review.

Is it safe to use in real work now?

Not yet. The core repository explicitly labels the system experimental and not accepted for production. Independent reviews, additional tests, and authorization are still required.

What are KITT and LRP?

KITT is the larger project's AI coordination system, while LRP is the reasoning-skills project. FNR aims to become one carefully controlled reasoning component inside that ecosystem, not a system with unrestricted powers.
